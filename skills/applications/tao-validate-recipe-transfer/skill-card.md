## Description: <br>
Port a published computer vision paper's official code and training recipe onto a customer's own dataset, or diagnose why such a transfer produced bad numbers. Splits the work into a Port phase — where the authors' released checkpoint and reported metric provide external ground truth — and a Transfer phase, where that ground truth disappears and every recipe field must be classified as transfer-invariant, scale-dependent, or dataset-derived. <br>

This skill is ready for commercial/non-commercial use. <br>

## Use Case: <br>
Developers and ML engineers use this skill when reproducing a CV paper, fine-tuning a published detection/segmentation/classification/keypoint model on customer data, or running a post-mortem on a training run whose numbers look wrong or suspiciously good. It provides three modes — forward, post-mortem, and audit — a checkpoint-reproduction gate, a quantified source-versus-target domain gap report, a near-duplicate split-leakage check, a train/eval/export preprocessing parity assertion, and a seven-rung verification ladder that fails cheap before real GPU hours are spent. <br>

### Deployment Geography for Use: <br>
Global <br>

## Known Risks and Mitigations: <br>
Risk: A recipe copied from a paper onto a different dataset can produce a plausible-looking but inflated metric, because an implementation bug and a genuine domain mismatch present identical symptoms once the dataset changes. <br>
Mitigation: The Phase A checkpoint gate, the split-leakage and preprocessing-parity scripts, the deliberately chosen operating point, and the mandatory "Known risks" report section keep every reported number attributable and auditable. <br>

Risk: Research repositories and their released weights frequently carry research-only, non-commercial, or copyleft terms that differ between code, weights, and backbone. <br>
Mitigation: Phase 0 makes the three-way licence check a hard gate before any porting work begins. <br>
